807 metres up, looking over the capital of the western highlands

In Tlemcen, you arrive through doorways: the carved gate of Sidi Boumediène, the walls of the Mechouar, and the arcades of the Great Mosque.

The hotel follows the same idea. Pointed bays line its façades, and its galleries rest on lobed arches, with one room opening into the next. The meeting rooms are named after the city's old names: Mechouar, Altava, Pomaria and Setti.

Alcazar means "the castle". It also sounds like Al Karaz, the cherry the region is known for.

The Story

From Renaissance to Alcazar

The hotel opened in April 2011, when Tlemcen was Capital of Islamic Culture. It first flew the Renaissance flag and was Marriott International's first property in Algeria, with 204 Arab-Moorish rooms and suites on the Lalla Setti plateau.

In 2026 it reopened as Alcazar Tlemcen, managed by SIH.

Rooms & Suites

There are 180 rooms and 24 suites. Every one has Alcazar bedding, a marble bathroom, air conditioning, a flat-screen TV, fast Wi-Fi, a minibar, a safe, a desk and a tea and coffee tray.

The Destination

The Safeer Programme

Instead of a classic concierge desk, the Safeer team gives you personal tips for exploring Tlemcen: monuments, craft workshops, quiet corners and cultural walks. About 60% of Algeria's Arab-Islamic heritage is in this city.
