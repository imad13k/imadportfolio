    Sources and method

    Meridian checks the public feeds of the publishers below every 20 seconds, or less often where a publisher asks for that, and sends new stories to this page as soon as they are found. A story can only appear after its publisher adds it to the feed. Headlines and short summaries belong to them, and every story links to the original article.

      Local AI

      • The model doesn't fetch news. Meridian's server fetches the feeds, then the model reads each new story on this computer. Nothing is sent to an outside AI service.
      • It sets country, topic and importance, and writes the Live brief from the current headlines. It can be wrong, so check the linked article before relying on it.

      Echo

      • Echo shows what was reported around a price move, from an hour before it began to when it ended. Rings on the charts mark the largest moves.
      • Timing is not proof of cause. Stories can overlap with a move by chance, and the real reason may not be in these feeds.

      Story mode

      • Stories replays recent conflict reports on the globe: missiles, drones, strikes, attacks and military activity, sorted from each headline's words.
      • The animations are illustrations. Feeds name a country, not an exact place, a launch site or a route, so nothing on the globe is a real track.
      • Auto plays new reports by themselves, only when you have not touched the page for a little while.

      Market prices

      • Companies shows large listed companies in seven sectors, from the same delayed source. A tile shows how many headlines name the company; click it to read them.
      • The gold, WTI and Brent charts are futures prices from Yahoo Finance, which runs behind the exchanges. The current delay is shown above the charts.
      • Gold spot comes from gold-api.com and is usually under a minute or two old; its age is shown on the card. It differs a little from the futures price.
      • Prices are for information only and not for trading.

      How to read the map

      • Shading counts coverage, not danger. A dark red country is one these outlets are writing about a lot, which also reflects where they are based.
      • Country and topic tags are automatic, matched from names and keywords in the text. Most are right, and some will be wrong.
      • The same story from several outlets counts once, grouped when their headlines closely match.
      • Links join countries named in the same headline. They show that two countries were mentioned together, not how they relate.
      • Borders come from Natural Earth and don't imply a position on any disputed territory.
